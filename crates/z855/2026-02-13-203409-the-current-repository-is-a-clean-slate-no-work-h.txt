❯ Are you familiar with Z85 encoding? (For quesitons like this, I want a    
  short answer of a few sentences, don't jump into long detailed            
  explanations unless requested.)                                           

⏺ Yes. Z85 is a binary-to-text encoding from the ZeroMQ project. It encodes 4
  bytes into 5 printable ASCII characters using an 85-character alphabet, giving
   it 25% overhead (more efficient than Base64's 33%). The alphabet is chosen to
   avoid characters that need escaping in most programming contexts.

❯ I know the Z85 specification only defines it over 4-byte blocks, but I've 
   seen many implementations which support arbitrary-length inputs. I       
  believe they use the same approach as unpadded base64. Is that correct?   

⏺ Yes, that's correct. For trailing bytes (1, 2, or 3), they encode into the
  minimum number of Z85 characters needed to represent them (2, 3, or 4
  characters respectively). The decoder infers the original byte count from the
  encoded length, just like unpadded base64.

❯ I know that Z85 encoding and decoding is less efficient than base64       
  because it requires division and not just bit-shifting, but it's still    
  relatively simple to implement, is that correct?                          

⏺ Yes. Encoding is repeated divide-by-85 and modulo to get alphabet indices.
  Decoding is multiply-accumulate with powers of 85. Straightforward loops, just
   slower than base64's bit manipulation.
